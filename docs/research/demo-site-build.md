# Sell guided demo sites as owned assets

## Executive summary

SureGround should add one new Specialty offer, the **Guided Demo Site Build**: a custom-coded, customer-shareable demo site of the client's product, built on story-driven synthetic data, versioned by industry, segment or named customer, and instrumented with per-recipient engagement tracking that flows into the CRM. List it publicly as **"Starting at $7,500" for a 2 to 3 week Demo Pilot**, with a full **Demo Site Build from $15,000**, a **Demo Program from $30,000**, and add-ons for industry or segment versions ($3,500), named-customer versions ($1,500) and **Demo Upkeep from $2,000 per month**. The pain is well documented, if mostly by vendors: SEs average **7 demos a week at about 3.6 hours each for a standard demo and 7 hours for a custom one**, and only **39% of SE teams have dedicated demo-environment resourcing** ([Navattic, State of Demo Automation 2026](https://www.navattic.com/report/state-of-demo-automation-2026)), while **69% build demos DIY or with no demo software at all** ([Reprise, State of Demos 2026](https://www.reprise.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/State-of-Demos-2026.pdf)). The platforms that address this run **$30,000 to $130,000 a year at the enterprise tier** and still need someone to build, personalize and maintain the demos ([Rep](https://www.meetrep.ai/blog/best-demo-automation-platforms-2026-the-complete-comparison); [HowdyGo](https://www.howdygo.com/blog/interactive-product-demo-comparison)), and the consultants who do that work cluster at **$900 to $1,500 per platform-built tour** ([Navattic Demo Consultants](https://www.navattic.com/demo-consultants/demo-dash)). That leaves an underserved middle: mid-market B2B SaaS teams with 2 to 10 SEs, no demo engineer, and a need for realistic data, vertical versions and deal-level engagement signals, sold by a former senior SE who also writes the React. The offer slots in beside the Answer Engine as a second B2B-seller Specialty, carries the house risk terms (a fee credit, milestone billing and a written definition of "live"), and positions as an asset the client owns rather than another seat license. Every price below is a recommendation to test, not a validated market rate. The next step is a showcase demo, 12 to 15 conversations with SE leaders and one paid pilot before the offer earns a spot on the home page.

A note on sourcing used throughout: figures marked **(vendor)** come from demo-software vendors or their competitors and are directional, often correlational; **(third-party)** means a non-vendor source; **(estimate)** means SureGround's own synthesis or arithmetic, not a published number.

## Build on React, story-driven synthetic data and first-party tracking

### The site

Build each demo as a **Next.js and TypeScript app**, styled with Tailwind and a component kit tuned to the client's design tokens, and deployed as a mostly static site on Vercel or the client's own host. A guided flow is a typed step config (screen, highlighted element, caption, next action), so SEs can reorder steps or rewrite captions by editing JSON rather than code. Variants are config layers over one base: an industry layer (terminology, dataset, narrative), a segment layer (SMB versus enterprise emphasis) and a named-customer layer (logo, colors, account names, use case), resolved from the URL, for example /demo/healthcare or /d/acme-7f3k. This is what makes the $3,500 industry and $1,500 customer prices profitable: each new version is mostly data and copy on a shared base. The client owns the repository at handover. Two guardrails matter. Build only from the client's own product and design assets, under a written license in the SOW, and never reuse an employer's UI (Feedonomics, FullStory, Shogun) in SureGround's showcase, since republishing another company's interface is a contractual question ([Ciela](https://ciela.ai/blogs/navattic-pricing-for-a-one-person-agency)).

### The synthetic data

The method combines two documented patterns. Salesforce's internal Org Data Generator has LLMs propose the hidden "latent variables" behind a dataset from the schema and a written use case, validates the output against platform rules, and has overtaken its older Snowfakery recipes in monthly requests at Salesforce ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/org-data-generator/)). A Databricks engineer described the data in plain English, had an LLM write the generation scripts with trends, seasonality and realistic anomalies, and kept the environment fresh with a **rolling timeline running from a year back to a month forward** ([Databricks](https://community.databricks.com/t5/technical-blog/building-realistic-environments-with-terraform-llms-and-data/ba-p/96697)). SureGround's pipeline should run: a story spec from SE interviews (personas, the problem, the before-and-after inflection point); an LLM-drafted data spec of entities, distributions and latent drivers; generation in Python with Faker or NVIDIA's open-source NeMo Data Designer ([GitHub](https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/DataDesigner)), or Tonic Fabricate at $0 to $29 per month ([Tonic pricing](https://tonic.ai/pricing)); validation scripts for referential integrity and story beats; and output to typed JSON fixtures. Store dates as offsets from "today" and resolve them at render time, so a static site never shows last quarter's activity, which directly addresses the staleness problem TestBox describes ([TestBox](https://www.testbox.com/post/why-demo-data-goes-stale-in-days-and-how-to-fix-it)). Tooling cost is near zero; the value is the story, the schema mapping and the per-industry base packs, starting with an ecommerce catalog-and-orders pack that draws on David's Feedonomics feed work. Reprise calls exposing real customer data in a demo a "major data privacy violation" ([Reprise](https://www.reprise.com/resources/blog/the-pros-and-cons-of-demoing-from-a-production-environment)), so the "no customer records are used" FAQ is a selling point, but the compliance link to HIPAA or GDPR was not sourced in this research and should be stated as plain risk reduction, not a compliance claim.

### The engagement analytics

Track through **tokenized share links**, one per recipient or per deal, rather than reverse-IP lookup, so every view maps to a named opportunity. Capture first-party events (open, step reached, completion, time on step, forward to a new viewer, CTA click) with PostHog or a small Vercel function writing to Postgres, then push a summary to the CRM as timeline activity (HubSpot first, since 66% of interactive-demo integrations are HubSpot ([Navattic](https://navattic.com/report/state-of-the-interactive-product-demo-2026))) and send Slack alerts to the AE and SE on first view, completion and new-viewer events. A simple share prompt inside the demo ("send this to a colleague") creates the multi-threading signal that Consensus and Navattic sell as buying-group discovery. The monthly report should cover view rate per link sent, completion, new viewers per deal and the gap between link sent and first view. Instrumenting every build this way lets clients test, on their own pipeline, the vendor claims about faster cycles rather than taking them on faith. Tracking named people needs care: put a short notice in the demo footer, set no third-party cookies, and give the client a one-paragraph data-handling note, which the Pilot Sprint already promises as a standard deliverable ([Pilot Sprint](https://sureground.tech/services/pilot-sprint)).

An **optional AI demo guide**, a chat panel grounded in the client's docs that answers questions inside the demo, is a natural later add-on because every platform now markets AI demo agents and SureGround already builds knowledge assistants. Leave it off the launch page until a pilot client asks for it.

| Commodity price pressure | Freelancers sell platform tours at $1,000 and Fiverr at $30 ([Fiverr](https://fiverr.com/enoch_mic/create-interactive-product-video-demo-on-supademo-navattic-and-layerpath-video)) | Never sell "a demo"; sell the data story, versions and deal analytics |
| Platforms move down-market with AI agents | AI-generated demos could commoditize basic builds within a year or two | Lead with narrative and data design, which AI tools still need a person to direct; add the AI guide when demand appears |
| Tracking privacy | Identifying individual viewers raises disclosure questions, especially for EU recipients | Footer notice, tokenized links, no third-party cookies, data-handling note in every build |
| Evidence quality | Nearly every ROI figure here is vendor-published and correlational | Keep statistics off the public page; measure each client's own results |
| Solo capacity | Three concurrent builds would saturate one person | Cap active builds at two; Upkeep clients get scheduled monthly slots |

### The validation plan

| Weeks | Step | Pass signal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 2 | Build a SureGround showcase demo: a fictional B2B product with healthcare, ecommerce and fintech versions, tokenized links and a live engagement dashboard | Demo completes in under 4 minutes; one named-customer version produced in a day |
| 1 to 3 | Publish the detail page and cards; add the "Demo build" contact topic and Related-offer links | Page live and linked from Answer Engine and Fractional SE |

| 12+ | Add the card to the home Specialty block, publish the case study, and consider applying to Navattic's consultant directory for the Platform pack | Two paying clients and one public result |

Conversation questions should test the riskiest assumptions directly: who owns demo upkeep today and how many hours it takes; whether the team already pays for a platform and what it fails to show; whether buyers ever forward demos internally; and whether a one-time owned build or a seat license is easier to get approved. If fewer than 3 of 15 leaders want pilot pricing, the likeliest pivots are either repackaging as the cheaper Platform data and story pack for teams already on a tool, or folding demo builds into the Fractional AI Solutions Engineer's build slot rather than selling them standalone.

## Conclusion

The research points to a clear wedge, not a crowded lane: the platforms are consolidating upmarket and toward AI agents, the freelancers are competing on price per tour, and the work in between (designing believable data, writing the narrative a buyer can follow without an SE, producing versions by vertical and account, and turning views into deal signals) is labor that most teams have nobody assigned to do. That work matches a former senior SE who can also ship React better than it matches either a tool or a tour builder, and selling it as an owned, fixed-fee asset lets SureGround compete against license renewals rather than hourly rates.

The bigger strategic point is what this does to SureGround's shape. With the Answer Engine, the Fractional AI Solutions Engineer and now a demo build, the B2B-seller side of the site becomes a coherent "help SaaS teams win deals" cluster that may prove easier to sell than SMB AI adoption, because its buyers already have budget lines for exactly this pain. The validation plan should therefore measure not just whether this one offer sells, but whether SE leaders respond faster than small-business owners do; that answer should shape where the home page points next year.
